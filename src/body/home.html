Learn to hear the bahr

In Urdu poetry, a bahr is the underlying rhythmic pattern that an entire ghazal adheres to — a sequence of long and short syllables. This app teaches you to hear it, see it, and scan it for yourself.

How to read the marks

Every scan marks each syllable long (=, two letters) or short (–, one letter), and a flexible syllable (x) may go either way, as the meter needs. These are Pritchett’s terms (Handbook §1.5 ↗). Here is the key, and one line marked out:

flexible
A syllable in a word that can scan long or short, like ہے or میں. The meter of the line decides.
x
The same idea in a meter pattern: a slot in the bahr itself where either weight is allowed. A flexible syllable fills it.
extra
One short syllable the meter doesn’t count, allowed at a line’s end. It is left out of the pattern.

Words we use

miṣraʿ
A single line or hemistich of poetry.
sheʿr
A complete, self-contained two-line couplet.
baḥr
The meter or rhythmic pattern that a poem follows.
rukn
A metrical foot (e.g. mafāʿīlun, fāʿilātun).
flexible
A syllable that may be scanned long or short, marked x.
dum / da
Vocal mnemonics for long (=) and short (–) syllables.
