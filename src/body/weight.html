Weight

How long each syllable is — the raw material of every meter.

Words we use

miṣraʿ
A single line or hemistich of poetry.
sheʿr
A complete, self-contained two-line couplet.
baḥr
The meter or rhythmic pattern that a poem follows.
rukn
A metrical foot (e.g. mafāʿīlun, fāʿilātun).
dum / da
Vocal mnemonics for long (=) and short (–) syllables.

Letters & weight

Three rules. A syllable is two letters or one. It starts with a consonant if it can. The split follows pronunciation.

The weight law. Two letters → long (=). One letter → short (–).

Invisible to meter: zer/zabar/pesh, the aspiration ھ, the nasal ں. Counts: hamza ء and ع as full consonants; tashdīd doubles its letter; آ is two letters.

Special cases

A few words split differently than the three rules alone would predict.

Bending

Flexibility in Urdu prosody. Many common monosyllables (کو، سے، کا، ہے، میں، بھی، نے، ہوں، یہ، وہ، جو، تو) can count as long (=) or short (–) according to the requirement of the meter.

Final vowels before vowels, the Persian iẓāfat connector (ِ / -e), and the conjunction o ("and") bridge words or alter lengths.
