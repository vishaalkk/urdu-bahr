Khalil’s Circles & Meter Calculation

Khalil ibn Ahmad al-Farahidi (8th century CE) discovered that classical poetic meters are generated by five circular rhythmic loops. Rotating around the loop generates different base meters. Combining the rhythm with line length and ending modifications calculates the bahr’s full name and selects its classical verse.

Why Poets Mix Sālim and Maḥẕūf in One Ghazal (The Scanner Sibling Rule)

Within a single ghazal, poets freely mix ziḥāf variations of the same base bahr. The first couplet might end intact (Sālim), while subsequent couplets trim the final foot (Maḥẕūf). Because both originate from the same circle rhythm, our scanner automatically recognizes and matches all permitted ziḥāf siblings of a bahr.
